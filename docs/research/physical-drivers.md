# Physical drivers of change

The challenge asks for hypotheses about the physical drivers behind observed
changes. The hypotheses below come from the cases and notes in the notebooks.
They are hypotheses, not validated conclusions.

## Seasonal flooding (La Mojana, Depresión Momposina, Colombia)

- **Driver:** La Mojana and the Momposina depression flood every rainy season
  (October to November).
- **SAR signature:** open water gives low HH; flooded vegetation raises HH by
  double bounce.
- **Context:** JRC GSW separates permanent water from new flooding; DSWx-S1 gives
  a C-band comparison on the same dates.
- Specific 2025-2026 flood events were not verified.

## River flood (Kruger / Limpopo)

- **Driver:** the flood of 2026-01-12 over the Kruger Park and Limpopo plain, for
  which NISAR published a flood map.
- **SAR signature:** L-band detects water under the canopy; optical DSWx-HLS is
  cloudy in January, showing the advantage of radar.

## Groundwater subsidence (Mexico City)

- **Driver:** over-extraction of the aquifer, reported at more than 2 cm/month.
- **SAR signature:** deformation measured by InSAR, not by backscatter; no
  tile layer shows it. OPERA DISP-S1 or NISAR InSAR are needed.

## Vegetation loss (Central Valley, Rondônia)

- **Driver:** fire, logging, drought or landslides. OPERA DIST labels describe
  vegetation loss but not its cause.
- **SAR signature:** loss of canopy reduces HV volume scattering.
- **Context:** Hansen GFC historical loss, OPERA DIST-ANN/DIST-ALERT.

## Earthquakes

- **Driver:** M7+ events in the NISAR window: Colombia M7.4 (2026-08-10), Flores
  M7.8 (2026-08-14), Panama M7.7 (2026-10-09), Tibet M7.0 (2026-10-22).
- **SAR signature:** before/after backscatter change from landslides and damage;
  relief (hillshade, DEM) explains shadow and layover in mountains.

## Anthropogenic drivers

Human activity (agriculture, industry) can act alongside natural drivers; the
notebooks do not analyze it. Land-cover context (ESA WorldCover) helps interpret polarimetry over
forest, crops and urban areas.
